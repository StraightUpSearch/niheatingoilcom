**The call: don’t turn NI Heating Oil into another generic “heating oil price” content site. Turn it into the **best local transaction/data layer for NI heating oil**.**

You already have the right core product: postcode → tank size → supplier comparison. The problem is that the current site is presenting that asset too much like a landing page and not enough like a **local search database + commercial marketplace**.

And the SERP has already shown you the threat: **NI Living** and **CheapOilNI** are building postcode/location inventory around essentially the same intent. ([NI Living][1])

Your supplied Local SEO manual backs the architecture I’d use here: dedicated commercial/service pages beat mass generic location pages, and programmatic pages only make sense where there is genuine search demand, data, differentiation and monetisation. 

## 1. Your biggest opportunity: own the postcode searches

Your homepage currently says:

> “Enter your BT postcode to get live quotes…”

That's good CRO.

But SEO-wise, you're leaving a **huge amount of searchable inventory** on the table.

Your competitor is already doing things like:

* `/postcode/bt7/`
* `/postcode/bt23/`
* `/postcode/bt15/`
* `/postcode/bt8/`

with actual supplier prices on the page. ([CheapOilNI][2])

I'd build:

```text
/heating-oil-prices/
├── /belfast/
├── /derry/
├── /lisburn/
├── /bangor/
├── /newry/
├── /newtownabbey/
├── /armagh/
├── /ballymena/
├── /coleraine/
├── /omagh/
│
└── /postcode/
    ├── /bt1/
    ├── /bt2/
    ├── /bt3/
    ├── ...
    └── /btxx/
```

But **do not simply generate 80 pages with the same paragraph and swap BT1 → BT2**.

The page needs actual data.

### Every postcode page should contain

**H1**

> Heating Oil Prices in BT7

Then:

* current 300L price
* current 500L price
* current 900L price
* cheapest supplier
* average price
* price per litre
* number of suppliers
* last updated timestamp
* delivery availability
* supplier comparison table
* local towns covered
* historical price chart
* NI average comparison
* "how much could you save?" calculation
* CTA to run the exact postcode comparison

And crucially:

> **BT7 is not just a keyword. It is a data entity.**

That's the difference between a useful programmatic site and 500 shit doorway pages.

The current Consumer Council data gives you a perfect benchmark layer because it publishes NI and council-area pricing for 300L, 500L and 900L. ([Consumer Council][3])

# 7. Monetisation: don't rely on ads

This is the part I'd change most aggressively.

You have a **transactional lead-generation asset**, not a blog.

I'd test four revenue models.

## A. Supplier referral / lead fee — #1

Supplier gets a qualified customer.

You charge:

* £X per qualified lead
* or £X per completed order
* or percentage of gross order value
* or monthly subscription for preferred placement

This is the obvious model.

Example:

> **Get quote**

If Supplier A pays £5–£20 for a qualified customer, suddenly your SEO traffic has an actual economic value.

Test it.

Don't assume the number.

# 18. The biggest technical thing I'd check

I'd want to inspect:

### Indexation

* How many `/results?postcode=...` URLs exist?
* Are they indexable?
* Are they canonical?
* Are there duplicate combinations?
* Are all tank-size variants generating URLs?
* Are parameter URLs getting indexed?
* Are there orphan postcode pages?

This matters because your current architecture uses:

```text
/results?postcode=BT1&volume=500
```

That can be excellent as a **functional application URL** but potentially horrible as the primary SEO architecture.

I'd create clean SEO URLs:

```text
/heating-oil-prices/belfast/
/heating-oil-prices/bt1/
```

and let the interactive comparison application sit behind that.

Don't let Google build an index of:

```text
?postcode=BT1&volume=300
?postcode=BT1&volume=500
?postcode=BT1&volume=900
?postcode=BT1&volume=1000
...
```

for every combination.

That's how you accidentally build a massive shitpile.

The supplied indexation SOP is very explicit that clean, deliberate URLs should form the search inventory rather than every parameter-driven URL.

## The strategic position I'd take

You don't need to become **the biggest heating oil website in Northern Ireland**.

You need to become:

> **the database people use when deciding where and when to buy heating oil in Northern Ireland.**

That gives you four compounding assets:

**1. Search inventory** — postcode × location × supplier × tank size
**2. Proprietary data** — current + historical pricing
**3. Entity authority** — NI heating oil comparison brand
**4. Commercial network** — suppliers paying for qualified demand

That's a much better business than an SEO blog with some AdSense slapped on it.

And there's a particularly nice strategic gap: the Consumer Council already owns **consumer trust/information**, while the suppliers own **transactions**. Your opportunity is to own the **commercial comparison layer between the two**. The NI government itself currently tells consumers to compare suppliers and points them towards the Consumer Council's price checker. ([Department for Communities][7])

**If this were my asset, I'd put the next serious chunk of development budget into the postcode/data/lead-gen layer — not another 50 blog posts.**

If you give me the **GSC export + current rankings + GA4/lead data**, I can turn this into a page-by-page attack plan with **exact URLs to create, consolidate, noindex, internally link and monetise**, rather than keeping it at strategy level.
